# Deviations from the Blueprint

`docs/PRD.md` and `docs/strategy.md` describe the product as originally
specified. They are deliberately left alone: they are the blueprint, not a
description of the build. This file is the honest record of where the shipped
code disagrees with them, and what would make us close the gap.

Everything below was specified, considered, and cut as premature - not
forgotten, not broken. Each entry states the trigger that would justify building
it.

---

## Cut features

### Radar section

**Blueprint**: a labelled `Section.RADAR` holding low-trust and preprint items,
capped at 2 slots inside the 12-slot budget.

**Shipped**: preprints and items whose `source_trust` subscore is <= 2 fail the
selection gates (`ExclusionReason.PREPRINT`, `ExclusionReason.UNRELIABLE_SOURCE`)
and are dropped. There is no Radar section and no `Section.RADAR` enum member.

**Why**: a section explicitly labelled "we are not sure about this" costs reader
attention and editorial review time on the material least likely to change
anything in a studio. Demoting weak items still puts them in front of the reader.

**Note**: `Tier.RADAR` still exists and is unrelated. It is a *source registry
tier* for low-trust outlets (Bambino Gesu, Meyer, Gaslini, UPPA, Medico e
Bambino) whose items are triangulation material.

**Trigger to build**: readers asking for a "what is coming" watchlist, or a
recurring pattern of items we had to drop that turned out to matter weeks later.

### Decision Cards

**Blueprint**: `DecisionCard` per device/POCT item - technology, evidence level,
sensitivity/specificity, cost, reimbursement, a mandatory "when NOT to test" box,
and a `Recommendation` verdict of adopt / evaluate / not_priority.

**Shipped**: nothing. `oykos.llm.decision_card`, the `DecisionCard` model and
the `Recommendation` enum are gone. Device items use the same 5-block format as
everything else.

**Why**: the card asks the LLM for around fifteen structured clinical and
economic fields, most of which are not present in the source document. That is a
hallucination surface with a medico-legal blast radius, and every card would
need a specialist reviewer we do not have.

**Trigger to build**: a named clinical reviewer who owns device appropriateness,
plus a curated evidence source (HTA reports, IVDR performance studies) that
actually contains the fields rather than asking a model to invent them.

### A/B subject line testing

**Blueprint**: two subject variants per issue, `AB_TEST_PERCENT` of subscribers
getting variant B, tracked via `subscriber.ab_group` and `subject_variant`.

**Shipped**: one subject line per issue, generated by the triage model.

**Why**: statistical significance on an open-rate difference needs a list far
larger than the current one, and Apple Mail Privacy Protection has made open
rate an unreliable metric anyway. The test would have produced noise.

**Trigger to build**: a list large enough for a weekly test to reach
significance, and a metric more reliable than opens - click-through on the
"Leggi online" link is the obvious candidate now that WordPress publishing exists.

---

### Embedding-based semantic dedup

**Blueprint**: "dedup via text hash + similarity embedding", with an `embedding`
column and pgvector as the production upgrade path.

**Shipped**: `oykos.ingestion.dedup` does canonical URL matching, then
`difflib.SequenceMatcher` title similarity at `TITLE_SIMILARITY_THRESHOLD = 0.85`
over a 28-day window. `USE_EMBEDDING_DEDUP`, `EMBEDDING_MODEL` and the
`embedding` column are gone.

**Why**: institutional sources republish under near-identical titles, which
string similarity catches perfectly well. Embeddings meant an API call per
ingested item, a vector to store, and a similarity sweep - real cost and real
operational surface for a marginal recall gain we could not demonstrate.

**Trigger to build**: measured duplicate leakage into published issues that
title similarity provably cannot catch - typically the same story covered by two
sources under genuinely different headlines.

### Referral system

**Blueprint**: `/refer/{code}` landing page, `referral_code`, `referred_by` and
`referral_count` on the subscriber.

**Shipped**: nothing. Growth is word of mouth and the public landing page.

**Why**: a referral loop is a growth mechanic for a product with proven
retention. Building the loop before knowing whether readers stay is optimising
the wrong end of the funnel.

**Trigger to build**: a stable weekly engaged rate over a meaningful number of
issues, plus readers actually forwarding issues unprompted.

---

### Archive full-text search

**Blueprint**: search across subject, week and body text on `/archive`.

**Shipped**: `/archive` lists sent issues newest first; `/archive/{week}` renders
one. The `q` parameter is gone.

**Why**: the archive holds a handful of issues. Search over a page that fits on
one screen is a maintenance cost with no user.

**Trigger to build**: enough issues that scanning the list stops working, or
readers asking "did you cover X?". Postgres full-text search on `newsletters` is
the cheap first step, and the WordPress site now indexes the same content anyway.

### Prefect orchestration

**Blueprint**: Prefect as the DAG orchestrator, with flows in
`oykos.pipeline.flows` behind the `prod` extra.

**Shipped**: plain async functions in `oykos.pipeline.{daily,weekly,runner}`,
driven by the CLI and cron. There is no `flows.py`, and Prefect is not used.

**Why**: the pipeline is three linear commands with no branching, no fan-out and
no cross-task dependency graph. A workflow engine would have added a server, a
database and a deployment model to schedule three cron entries. Retries live in
the OpenAI and httpx clients where they belong; failures are visible through
structured logs and `HEALTHCHECK_PING_URL`.

Both `README.md` and `.github/copilot-instructions.md` claimed Prefect was the
orchestrator. It never really was.

**Trigger to build**: genuine fan-out (per-source parallel branches with
independent retry and backfill), or a need to replay a single failed stage of a
past run without re-running the rest.

### PDF ingestion

**Blueprint**: a PDF connector for guidelines, circulars and consensus documents.

**Shipped**: nothing. `oykos.ingestion.pdf` is gone. `SourceType.PDF` remains in
the enum but no registry entry uses it.

**Why**: Italian institutional PDFs are scanned images, multi-column layouts and
tables as often as they are extractable text. Extraction quality was
unpredictable, and an unreliable quote breaks the verbatim guard that the whole
grounding model rests on. The landing page for each PDF is usually scrapeable and
carries the operative summary.

**Trigger to build**: a specific high-value source that publishes *only* as PDF
and whose PDFs are reliably text-layered.

### Automated evaluation harness

**Blueprint**: offline gold-set evaluation - NDCG@k on ranking, groundedness
rate, corrections rate.

**Shipped**: nothing. `oykos.observability.evaluation` is gone.
`oykos.observability.metrics.compute_quality_report` still runs per issue and
logs coverage gaps and ratio violations, and `review_decisions` still records
every editorial correction, so the corrections rate remains computable.

**Why**: NDCG needs a labelled gold set. Building one means an expert ranking
hundreds of items - the same expert whose time is better spent reviewing the
issues actually going out. The human review queue is the evaluation harness for
now, and it operates on real traffic.

**Trigger to build**: changing the scoring weights or the ranking algorithm.
Without a gold set there is no way to tell an improvement from a regression, so
this must exist *before* the first weight change.

### Auto-approval review policy

**Blueprint**: risk-based review - mandatory sign-off on the Top 3, prescriptive
items, privacy/ACN and device safety; stable 20% sampling on everything else.

**Shipped**: `oykos.processing.review_policy` is gone. `compose_newsletter` sets
`needs_human_review = True` on every selected item. An issue cannot be approved
until every item has a decision.

**Why**: an issue is 12 items and an editor reads all of them anyway. The
sampling policy added a code path whose only function was to let unreviewed
medical content reach readers.

**Trigger to build**: issue volume high enough that full review stops being
feasible - realistically, a daily cadence or several parallel editions.

**Related**: `AUTO_SEND_AFTER_HOURS` is not this. It defaults to `0` (wait
indefinitely), and when set it ships only the items that *were* cleared, dropping
the rest. It never sends unreviewed content.

### Geo caps removed entirely

**Blueprint**: a 70/30 Italy/foreign split on the final slots, deviating by up to
`MAX_GEO_DEVIATION` (2) until the issue reaches `MIN_VIABLE_TOTAL` (10) items.

**Shipped**: there is no geography quota at all. `MAX_GEO_DEVIATION`,
`MIN_VIABLE_TOTAL`, `relax_geo_caps`, `MAX_ITALY`, `MAX_FOREIGN` and
`ITALY_RATIO` are gone. `rank_and_select` caps only how many items may come from
a single source (2). Italian applicability is one weighted criterion worth 10%
inside the score.

**Why**: the editorial feedback of 2026-08-07 found the bot leading with a story
about intraosseous access in the critically ill child - clinically sound, aimed
at a hospital team. Geography was outranking audience fit in two ways at once: a
quota reserved slots for Italian items, and Italian applicability was applied
twice, as a criterion AND as a multiplier on the total. Every item now competes
on relevance to PLS practice.

A thin week still produces a shorter issue. Padding optimises for a number, not
for the reader, and the `EDITORIAL_HEADROOM` shortlist absorbs the common case of
a verification block shortening the issue.

---

### Actions are optional

**Blueprint and editorial guidelines v1.0**: every item ends with a single
priority action under a "Cosa fare ora" heading.

**Shipped**: an item may conclude that nothing changes. `ImplicationKind`
carries what the source justifies, and the label follows from it. For a
non-institutional source the conclusion must use one of the wordings listed in
guidelines v2.0 section 10; anything else is dropped in code.

**Why**: guidelines v2.0 replaced the formula "cosa cambia, perché conta, cosa
fare adesso" precisely because it induces the writer to invent a behaviour to
propose. See `docs/editorial-guidelines.md`.

**Trigger to build**: repeated issues falling below the useful length purely
because of the geo caps, with good foreign material sitting unused in the
backlog.

## Summary

| Feature | Blueprint | Shipped | Reopens when |
|---------|-----------|---------|--------------|
| Radar section | Yes | Excluded outright | Readers want a watchlist |
| Decision Cards | Yes | No | A named clinical reviewer owns it |
| A/B subject lines | Yes | Single subject | List reaches significance |
| Semantic dedup | Yes | Title similarity 0.85 | Measured duplicate leakage |
| Referrals | Yes | No | Retention is proven |
| Archive search | Yes | List only | Archive outgrows one screen |
| Prefect | Yes | Async + cron | Real fan-out or stage replay |
| PDF ingestion | Yes | No | A PDF-only source with a text layer |
| Eval harness | Yes | Per-issue quality report | Before any scoring-weight change |
| Auto-approval | Yes | Full human review | Volume makes full review infeasible |
| Geo-cap relaxation | Yes | Hard caps | Good foreign material sits unused |
